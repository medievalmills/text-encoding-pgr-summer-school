# Text Encoding (HASS Summer School 2026)

### Wait, where am I?
Hi! You're on GitHub: it's a site used by techy nerds (such as myself) to share files and other documents. Specifically, you're in the 'repository' for the Text Encoding training session for PGRs, which will be delivered as part of the Research Methods Summer School at the University of Exeter on 15th September 2026.

### Wait, 'text encoding'?
Yep! Specifically, [TEI-P5](https://tei-c.org/release/doc/tei-p5-doc/en/html/index.html). This session is designed as a very basic introduction to working with TEI (and XML more generally): what it is, how it works, and why you might want to consider using it in your projects. The session will cover some of the key concepts in TEI: typographic vs. semantic / descriptive markup, tagging, validity and well-formedness, and how all this fits into the broader ecosystem of digital editions, projects, and humanities scholarship more generally.

### Wait, and who are you?
Hi! I'm [Edward](https://edwardmills.uk), a postdoc at Exeter. I'll be running the session.

### Great. What do I need to do before the session?
Not much, really! Just a few things, which (if you do them beforehand) will save us quite a bit of time ahead of the session itself.
1. **Download this repository** as a ZIP file, by clicking on the green 'Code' button and selecting the 'Download ZIP' button.
1. **Open the ZIP file** and save it somewhere easy to access. I'd recommend your desktop. *Important: don't move the files outside the folder, wherever you decide to put it!*
1. **Download oXygen XML Editor** from [this link](https://www.oxygenxml.com/xml_editor/download_oxygenxml_editor.html) and **install it** wherever you usually put your applications. Note that when you open it, you'll be prompted to add a license key. I'll be providing this on the day, but if you want to experiment a little bit in advance, you can request a 30-day trial license by putting in an email address.
